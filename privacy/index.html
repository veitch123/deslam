DeSlammatory: Privacy Policy

Last updated 28 September 2026.

DeSlammatory (formerly Deslam the News) is an app and Safari extension for iPhone, iPad and Mac, made by James Veitch. It rewrites sensational wording in news headlines on your device, and marks every change so you can always see what the paper wrote.

The short version

DeSlammatory does not collect, store or share your personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. It rewrites headlines on your device. It downloads its daily list of rewritten headlines, the list of headlines the news site you are reading publishes for feed readers, and, unless you turn it off, the start of a clickbait article from the site you are reading; none of these carries anything about you. The only thing it ever sends is a report you choose to send about a headline, and only when you tap Send (below).

What it reads

To rewrite headlines, the Safari extension reads the text of the web pages you open in Safari while it is switched on. It does this on your device. It does not send page text anywhere, and it does not keep a record of the pages you visit. (If Apple Intelligence is on, it remembers its own rewrites for a week, on your device, as described below.)

Safari asks you to allow the extension on websites because it has to read and change the text of news pages. You choose where it can run in Safari's settings, and you can pause it on any site from its menu.

Ad-blocker pop-ups

Some websites cover the page with a request to switch off your ad blocker. DeSlammatory hides these on your device, and lets the page scroll again. To recognise one, it looks only at the pop-ups shown over the page, and at their words. It keeps nothing about them or the page, and sends nothing anywhere. The daily list (below) also carries short descriptions of such pop-ups, made by its maker, not by readers: the website, the pop-up's first few words, and what kind of element it is.

Apple Intelligence

If you turn on the optional Apple Intelligence setting, headlines are rewritten by Apple's on-device language model. The headlines stay on your device. Deslam does not use Apple's Private Cloud Compute or any other online service. So that a headline is not worked out twice, and the article it leads to opens with the same rewrite, Deslam remembers Apple Intelligence's answers for a week in the extension's storage on your device: the headline, its rewrite, and the address of the article the headline linked to. This never leaves your device, and older answers are deleted automatically.

Clickbait and the article behind it

This is on unless you turn off "Read the article to answer it" in the app. When it is on, Apple Intelligence is on, and a headline is clickbait (it holds back the point to make you click), Deslam may fetch the start of that headline's own article from the same website you are reading, so it can say plainly what the headline was hiding. The request goes only to that website, carries no cookies and no referrer, and the article text is used on your device and not kept. The website sees the request as it would any page load from your device.

The site's own list of headlines

To tell a page's headlines from the rest of its text, DeSlammatory may download the list of headlines a news site publishes for feed readers (its RSS or Atom feed), when the page you are reading names one. It fetches it only from the same website you are reading, at most once an hour for each site, with no cookies and no referrer, so the request carries nothing about you, and the website sees it as it would any request from your device. It keeps the headlines and their links on your device (for the 40 sites it fetched most recently) and sends them nowhere.

The daily edition

When Safari starts, when a web page starts loading or comes back into view, at most once a minute, DeSlammatory checks for a new list of the day's most-read headlines, rewritten by hand. It reads the list first from Apple's iCloud (CloudKit Web Services, api.apple-cloudkit.com, the app's public database, which holds only the list), and if that fails from https://raw.githubusercontent.com/veitch123/deslam/main/pack.json (or https://veitch123.github.io/deslam/pack.json). Apple's privacy policy is at https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/. The list is also hosted by GitHub. The "Update James's custom headlines" button in the menu fetches it instead from GitHub's API, https://api.github.com/repos/veitch123/deslam/contents/pack.json, which is up to date sooner. The request carries nothing about you or the pages you read, and no cookies. Like any website, GitHub can see the internet address the request came from; its privacy statement is at https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement. The list is signed, and Deslam ignores any list not signed by its maker. You can switch the daily rewrites off in Deslam's menu in Safari.

So that the list is on your iPhone or iPad before you open Safari, the DeSlammatory app also downloads the same signed list from Apple's iCloud (CloudKit's public database, which anyone can read and which holds only the list): when the app opens, when Apple's push service wakes it because a new list has been published, and when iOS runs its background refresh, which the app asks for in the early morning and the afternoon. On a Mac the app does this only while it is open. Nothing about you or your reading is sent. So that Apple can wake the app, the app asks iCloud to notify it about new lists (only if you are signed in to iCloud), and Apple keeps that request. It is filed under a random identifier Apple makes for this app, which does not tell James who you are, and it holds nothing but the request to be told about new lists. Apple handles iCloud and its push service under its own privacy policy, https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/. The list the app downloads is kept in the storage the app and extension share on your device, like the one the extension downloads. You can stop the background downloads by turning off Background App Refresh for DeSlammatory in Settings.

What it stores on your device

Deslam keeps your settings on your device: whether it is on, which sites it is paused on, whether changes are marked, whether Apple Intelligence is on, and which kinds of wording it changes, the latest daily list, and the news sites' own lists of headlines described above. The app and the extension share these settings through storage on your device that only Deslam can read. Copies of any reports you make (the last 200) are kept in the extension's storage on your device, including the address of the page you reported, with the times of the reports sent in the last hour (so that no more than five are sent in an hour). None of this leaves your device, except a report when you send it, and deleting the app deletes it.

Reporting a mistake

If you think DeSlammatory got a headline wrong, you can say so from the pop-up of the mark after the headline ("This is wrong") or from its menu in Safari ("Report", or "Report something wrong" for a headline it missed). Nothing is sent until you tap Send.

A report contains: the headline as the paper wrote it; DeSlammatory's version of it, and whether its rules, Apple Intelligence or its daily edition made that version; your own wording, if you changed it; anything you chose to write; the address of the page (without anything after a "?" or "#" in it, which can carry details about you) and the website's name; DeSlammatory's version number; and whether you are using an iPhone, iPad or Mac. It contains nothing else: not your name, email address, Apple ID or any other page you have visited.

When you tap Send, the report is stored in Apple's iCloud, in DeSlammatory's own CloudKit database, which is set so that only DeSlammatory's maker can read reports; other readers cannot. To store it, your device must be signed in to iCloud. CloudKit files each report under an identifier Apple makes for you for DeSlammatory alone (and notes which device sent it), which does not tell DeSlammatory's maker who you are; DeSlammatory does not read or use it. Apple handles iCloud under its own privacy policy, https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/. At most five reports are sent from a device in an hour.

If a report can't be sent (you aren't signed in to iCloud, you are offline, or you have sent five in the last hour), DeSlammatory offers to write it as an email for you instead, in your own mail app, or to copy it for you to paste. You can read and edit the email first, and nothing is sent unless you send it yourself.

Reports are used only to fix DeSlammatory's mistakes. Reports that have been dealt with are kept so the same mistake is not reported twice. You can ask for a report to be deleted at any time: privacy@veitch.me.

TestFlight

If you test Deslam through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share crash reports, and any feedback you choose to send through TestFlight, with the developer. Apple handles this under its own TestFlight terms and privacy policy.

Children

Deslam does not collect data from anyone, including children.
