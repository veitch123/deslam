DeSlammatory: Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 September 2026.

DeSlammatory (formerly Deslam the News) is an app and Safari extension for iPhone, iPad and Mac, made by James Veitch. It rewrites sensational wording in news headlines on your device, and marks every change so you can always see what the paper wrote.

The short version

DeSlammatory does not collect, store or share your personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. It rewrites headlines on your device. It downloads its daily list of rewritten headlines, and, only if you turn it on, the start of a clickbait article from the site you are reading; neither carries anything about you.

What it reads

To rewrite headlines, the Safari extension reads the text of the web pages you open in Safari while it is switched on. It does this on your device. It does not send page text anywhere, and it does not keep a record of the pages you visit. (If Apple Intelligence is on, it remembers its own rewrites for a week, on your device, as described below.)

Safari asks you to allow the extension on websites because it has to read and change the text of news pages. You choose where it can run in Safari's settings, and you can pause it on any site from its menu.

Ad-blocker pop-ups

Some websites cover the page with a request to switch off your ad blocker. DeSlammatory hides these on your device, and lets the page scroll again. To recognise one, it looks only at the pop-ups shown over the page, and at their words. It keeps nothing about them or the page, and sends nothing anywhere. The daily list (below) also carries short descriptions of such pop-ups, made by its maker, not by readers: the website, the pop-up's first few words, and what kind of element it is.

Apple Intelligence

If you turn on the optional Apple Intelligence setting, headlines are rewritten by Apple's on-device language model. The headlines stay on your device. Deslam does not use Apple's Private Cloud Compute or any other online service. So that a headline is not worked out twice, and the article it leads to opens with the same rewrite, Deslam remembers Apple Intelligence's answers for a week in the extension's storage on your device: the headline, its rewrite, and the address of the article the headline linked to. This never leaves your device, and older answers are deleted automatically.

Clickbait and the article behind it

This is off unless you turn on "Read the article to answer it" in the app. When it is on, Apple Intelligence is on, and a headline is clickbait (it holds back the point to make you click), Deslam may fetch the start of that headline's own article from the same website you are reading, so it can say plainly what the headline was hiding. The request goes only to that website, carries no cookies and no referrer, and the article text is used on your device and not kept. The website sees the request as it would any page load from your device.

The daily edition

When Safari starts, when a web page starts loading or comes back into view, at most once a minute, DeSlammatory checks for a new list of the day's most-read headlines, rewritten by hand, from https://raw.githubusercontent.com/veitch123/deslam/main/pack.json (or https://veitch123.github.io/deslam/pack.json if that fails). The list is hosted by GitHub. The "Update James's custom headlines" button in the menu fetches it instead from GitHub's API, https://api.github.com/repos/veitch123/deslam/contents/pack.json, which is up to date sooner. The request carries nothing about you or the pages you read, and no cookies. Like any website, GitHub can see the internet address the request came from; its privacy statement is at https://docs.github.com/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement. The list is signed, and Deslam ignores any list not signed by its maker. You can switch the daily rewrites off in Deslam's menu in Safari.

So that the list is on your iPhone or iPad before you open Safari, the DeSlammatory app also downloads the same signed list from Apple's iCloud (CloudKit's public database, which anyone can read and which holds only the list): when the app opens, when Apple's push service wakes it because a new list has been published, and when iOS runs its background refresh, which the app asks for in the early morning and the afternoon. On a Mac the app does this only while it is open. Nothing about you or your reading is sent. So that Apple can wake the app, the app asks iCloud to notify it about new lists (only if you are signed in to iCloud), and Apple keeps that request. It is filed under a random identifier Apple makes for this app, which does not tell James who you are, and it holds nothing but the request to be told about new lists. Apple handles iCloud and its push service under its own privacy policy, https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/. The list the app downloads is kept in the storage the app and extension share on your device, like the one the extension downloads. You can stop the background downloads by turning off Background App Refresh for DeSlammatory in Settings.

What it stores on your device

Deslam keeps your settings on your device: whether it is on, which sites it is paused on, whether changes are marked, whether Apple Intelligence is on, and which kinds of wording it changes, and the latest daily list. The app and the extension share these settings through storage on your device that only Deslam can read. Copies of any reports you make (the last 200) are kept in the extension's storage on your device, including the address of the page you reported. None of this leaves your device, and deleting the app deletes it.

Reporting a mistake

If you report a headline Deslam got wrong, it writes an email for you in your own mail app, or copies the report for you to paste. The report contains the headline as published, Deslam's version, what was changed and whether the rules or Apple Intelligence changed it, the address of the page, the version of Deslam, whether you are using Safari on a Mac, iPhone or iPad, and anything you choose to add. You can read and edit it first, and nothing is sent unless you send it yourself. If you do, it is used only to fix Deslam's mistakes, and you can ask for it to be deleted at any time.

TestFlight

If you test Deslam through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may share crash reports, and any feedback you choose to send through TestFlight, with the developer. Apple handles this under its own TestFlight terms and privacy policy.

Children

Deslam does not collect data from anyone, including children.
